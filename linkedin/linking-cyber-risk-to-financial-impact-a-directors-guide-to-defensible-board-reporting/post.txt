A board paper lands claiming cyber risk exposure of "$2.3 million" — precise, defensible, ready for ASIC scrutiny. But when you probe the methodology, it unravels. The calculation assumes perfect incident response, excludes regulatory penalties, and treats reputational damage as zero.

This isn't just poor analysis. It's a governance failure that exposes every director to personal liability under continuous disclosure obligations.

The problem runs deeper than bad math. Most cyber risk reports conflate technical vulnerabilities with business impact, leaving boards with numbers they cannot defend and decisions they cannot justify.

ASIC expects directors to demonstrate they've properly considered material risks. A cyber incident that costs $50 million while your board papers claimed $2.3 million exposure creates an indefensible position.

The solution requires forensic precision: mapping specific threat scenarios to quantified financial impacts using defensible methodologies that survive regulatory examination.

Because when the regulator asks how you knew the risk was manageable, "the CISO said so" won't protect you.

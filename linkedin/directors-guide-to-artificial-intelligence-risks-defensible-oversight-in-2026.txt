A director at a major retailer asked their CRO last month: "If our AI pricing algorithm discriminates against protected groups, who goes to jail — me or the data scientist?" The silence that followed revealed how few boards understand their personal liability for AI decisions.

Directors face criminal penalties under discrimination law regardless of whether bias was intentional. Your AI systems are making thousands of decisions daily — in hiring, lending, pricing, and customer service. Each decision creates potential director liability if it violates consumer protection or anti-discrimination statutes.

The "we didn't know our algorithm was biased" defence disappeared when algorithmic auditing became commercially available. Courts now expect boards to implement systematic AI risk controls, not delegate oversight to technical teams who lack legal training.

Your due diligence obligation extends to understanding which AI systems make decisions affecting people, how bias testing occurs, and whether your legal team reviews algorithmic impacts before deployment.

The question isn't whether your AI will face regulatory scrutiny — it's whether you can demonstrate you governed it properly when that scrutiny arrives.

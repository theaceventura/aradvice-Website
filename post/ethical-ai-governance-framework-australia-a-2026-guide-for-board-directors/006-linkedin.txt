Your board approved an AI strategy last quarter, but can you actually defend how that AI system makes decisions about customer creditworthiness? When pressed on the ethical framework governing your AI deployment, most directors discover they're operating on assumptions rather than defensible governance structures.

The Corporations Act doesn't pause for AI complexity. Your fiduciary duty to act with care and diligence applies whether the decision comes from a human analyst or an algorithmic model. Yet boards continue approving AI initiatives without establishing clear accountability mechanisms for algorithmic outcomes.

An ethical AI governance framework isn't about philosophical principles — it's about creating audit trails, defining decision boundaries, and ensuring you can explain to stakeholders exactly how your AI systems align with your stated values. This means establishing clear oversight of data inputs, model validation processes, and outcome monitoring that goes beyond technical performance metrics.

The boards that will thrive are those that treat AI governance as seriously as they treat financial controls. They're building frameworks now, before the inevitable moment when they need to explain an AI decision that went wrong.

You cannot govern what you cannot interrogate, and you cannot interrogate what you have not designed to be transparent.

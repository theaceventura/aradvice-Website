Your board approved a generative AI policy six months ago. Your executives report monthly on "AI governance metrics." Yet you still cannot answer this question: if your organisation's AI system causes material harm tomorrow, can you demonstrate you exercised reasonable care in your oversight?

The answer for most Australian boards is no. Policy documents and dashboard metrics create an illusion of control while actual AI risks compound daily beneath board visibility.

Directors who mistake AI governance theatre for genuine oversight face a stark reality: ASIC and AICD expectations demand you can articulate specific risk decisions, not just point to process documents.

A defensible governance framework requires three non-negotiable elements: granular visibility into AI risk exposures across your organisation, clear decision-making protocols that escalate material AI risks to board level, and evidence trails that demonstrate active board consideration of specific AI deployment decisions.

The organisations getting this right treat AI governance as a core board competency, not an IT compliance exercise.

Your duty of care doesn't pause while you learn about generative AI risks — it starts the moment your organisation deploys these systems.

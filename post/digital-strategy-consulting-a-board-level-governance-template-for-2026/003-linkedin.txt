A board paper lands on your desk: "Digital Transformation Strategy Update — Investment Required: $47M." The paper outlines cloud migration, AI implementation, and cybersecurity upgrades. But buried in appendix C is a single line about "assumed regulatory compliance" with no detail on which regulations or how compliance will be achieved.

Under the Corporations Act, directors have a duty to make informed decisions. Yet most digital strategy papers present technology choices without governance frameworks. They detail technical architectures but skip the control environments that make those architectures defensible.

The Cyber Security Act 2024 creates explicit obligations for critical infrastructure. AI governance is evolving rapidly. A $47M digital investment without clear governance isn't just poor strategy — it's potentially a breach of fiduciary duty.

When regulators ask how you governed digital risk, pointing to a vendor's assurance matrix won't suffice. They want board-level oversight frameworks, not technical specifications.

Your digital strategy consulting must address governance first, technology second — or you're governing blind.

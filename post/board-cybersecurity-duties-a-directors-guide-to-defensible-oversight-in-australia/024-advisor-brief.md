## What the article argues

Australian directors can no longer rely on high-level technical summaries or management assurances to satisfy their duty of care under Section 180 of the Corporations Act 2001. The full enforcement of the Cyber Security Act 2024 has raised the minimum standard for board-level oversight of digital risk, making personal accountability a live and present concern. Andy argues that only independent advisory provides the objective validation directors need to build a legally defensible record of oversight.

## How to respond -- key messages

**On whether the Act applies to their company:** The article does not distinguish by company size or sector, so Andy should acknowledge that scope varies and offer to map the client's specific obligations rather than give a blanket answer. The honest position is that the governance standard under Section 180 applies universally regardless of whether the Cyber Security Act 2024 catches them directly.

**On whether existing dashboards and a CISO are sufficient:** Andy should be direct that receiving information is not the same as interrogating it. Section 180 requires directors to question the underlying risk assumptions, not just accept what management presents, and a board that cannot demonstrate that questioning has occurred is exposed.

**On what a defensible record looks like:** Andy should describe concrete outputs -- board minutes that record specific questions asked, documented escalation thresholds, evidence of independent review of management assurances, and a cyber risk appetite statement that is formally reviewed. These are the artefacts a regulator or court would look for.

**On ASIC enforcement activity:** Andy should be careful here (see Watch-outs) and acknowledge that enforcement is evolving. The honest message is that the regulatory direction is clear even if specific director prosecutions under this framework are not yet numerous.

**On independent advisory:** Andy should position this not as an add-on but as the mechanism that closes the verification gap -- the board cannot self-certify that management assurances are reliable.

## Watch-outs

- **The article overstates certainty on personal director liability.** Section 180 is well established but its specific application to cybersecurity oversight has not been tested extensively in Australian courts. Andy should not imply that prosecution of directors for cyber failures under Section 180 is routine or imminent -- sophisticated clients will push back if that framing feels exaggerated.
- **The scope of the Cyber Security Act 2024 is not fully explained in the article.** The piece treats it as applying broadly to "all Australian entities" but the Act's obligations vary significantly by entity type, particularly for the ransomware reporting requirement. Andy should be ready to clarify scope rather than rely on the article's generalisation.
- **APRA CPS 234 is a regulated-entity standard.** The article positions it as a cross-industry benchmark, which is a reasonable advisory framing, but clients outside APRA-regulated sectors may reasonably ask why a banking standard is relevant to them. Andy should be prepared to explain the "benchmark, not obligation" distinction clearly.

SUBJECT: Cyber Security Act 2024: Your personal liability as a director

A board received a cybersecurity update last month, nodded through a clean dashboard, and signed off on the minutes with no record of what questions were asked or what evidence was tested. When a breach followed, that minute was the only document ASIC had to assess whether the directors had met their duty of care under Section 180 of the Corporations Act 2001. A finding that they had not is a personal finding, not a company finding, and it can end a directorship. The gap between receiving a management briefing and actually discharging your legal duty is where personal exposure sits.

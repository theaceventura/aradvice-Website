Consider a chair who is asked, mid-meeting, to sign off on the board's cybersecurity risk position. The CISO has presented a dashboard. Metrics are green. The chair approves. What that chair cannot show, if ASIC comes asking, is a single piece of documented evidence that the board independently tested the assumptions behind those green lights.

Section 180 of the Corporations Act 2001 does not exempt directors who relied on management assurances. It requires care and diligence, and the full enforcement of the Cyber Security Act 2024 has made that standard explicitly personal when digital risk is involved.

The practical problem is not access to information. Boards receive plenty of it. The problem is that technical dashboards are designed to summarise, not to expose the governance weaknesses that sit underneath them. A director who cannot probe the methodology behind a report is not exercising oversight. They are receiving it.

Defensible oversight produces a record. It documents the questions asked, the assurances sought, the independent verification obtained. That record is what distinguishes a board that governed from a board that was briefed.

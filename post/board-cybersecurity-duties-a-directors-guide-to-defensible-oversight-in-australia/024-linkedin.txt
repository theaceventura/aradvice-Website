Consider a chair who is asked, mid-board meeting, whether the company's incident response plan has been tested against a ransomware scenario. The CISO nods. The dashboard is green. The chair moves on. That nod is not governance, and under Section 180 of the Corporations Act 2001, it will not function as a defence.

The full enforcement of the Cyber Security Act 2024 has formalised something that was previously a matter of interpretation: cybersecurity oversight is now a personal accountability question for every director on the register. Relying on management assurances without the tools to verify them is not a governance posture, it is an exposure.

What defensible oversight actually requires is a structured record of inquiry, not a record of attendance. ASIC's evidence-based expectations now demand that directors demonstrate they asked the right questions, tested the answers, and understood what the responses meant for material risk. A green dashboard does not satisfy that standard.

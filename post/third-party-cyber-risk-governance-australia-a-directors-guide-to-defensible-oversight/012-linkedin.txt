A board receives their quarterly cyber report showing 847 third-party vendors have access to company systems. The security team rates 23 as "high risk" but admits they haven't conducted security assessments on 312 vendors in over 18 months. The chair asks: "Are we liable if one of these vendors causes a breach?"

Under Australian law, the answer is increasingly yes. Directors cannot delegate cyber risk oversight to third parties — they remain accountable for the consequences of vendor failures that damage their organisation or customers.

The challenge isn't just knowing which vendors pose risk. It's building governance frameworks that can demonstrate to ASIC or APRA that the board exercised reasonable care in overseeing third-party cyber arrangements. This requires moving beyond vendor questionnaires to active, ongoing risk assessment.

Boards need visibility into vendor security posture, contractual protections that actually transfer meaningful risk, and incident response plans that account for supplier failures. Without these elements, directors are governing a supply chain they cannot properly assess.

Your liability doesn't diminish because the risk sits with a third party — it multiplies because you've added another point of failure outside your direct control.

## How to respond — key messages

**On reporting adequacy**: Defensible cyber reporting requires clear linkage between technical risks and business outcomes, with regular updates that demonstrate active board oversight. Focus on materiality thresholds and business continuity impacts rather than technical details.

**On financial quantification**: Push for scenario-based financial modeling that includes direct costs, business disruption, regulatory penalties, and reputational impacts. Ensure management provides ranges and confidence intervals rather than false precision.

**On documentation and process**: Maintain detailed records of board discussions, decisions, and follow-up actions on cyber matters. Ensure risk appetite statements explicitly address cyber risks with measurable parameters.

**On reporting frequency and oversight**: Quarterly detailed updates with immediate notification for material incidents or threat changes. Board should receive executive summaries with detailed technical reports available as backup.

## Watch-outs

• Avoid overstating the precision possible in cyber risk quantification - most financial impact models involve significant uncertainty that should be acknowledged
• Be careful not to create unrealistic expectations about board-level cyber expertise - directors need to ask the right questions but aren't expected to be technical experts
• Recognize that regulatory expectations are evolving rapidly and guidance may become outdated quickly, particularly around the new Cyber Security Act requirements

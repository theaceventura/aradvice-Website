A director asked their CISO: "What's our residual risk after these controls?" The CISO responded with a heat map showing everything as "medium" or "low" but couldn't explain the methodology behind those ratings. When pressed on whether the board could rely on those assessments for decision-making, the room went quiet.

This scenario reveals a fundamental governance problem: CISOs often present risk assessments that look authoritative but lack defensible foundations. Directors accept these reports because challenging technical content feels uncomfortable, yet the Corporations Act requires informed decision-making regardless of subject complexity.

The solution isn't learning cybersecurity — it's learning to ask the right governance questions. "How was this risk level determined?" "What assumptions underpin this assessment?" "If this assessment is wrong, what's our exposure?" These questions work because they focus on process and accountability, not technical detail.

Directors who master this approach transform CISO reporting from performance theatre into genuine strategic input. They create environments where technical leaders must think like business leaders, translating complexity into decision-ready information.

The real test of cyber governance isn't whether you understand the technology — it's whether you can defend your oversight decisions to a regulator.

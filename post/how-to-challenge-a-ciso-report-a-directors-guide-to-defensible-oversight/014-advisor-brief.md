## What the article argues

The article argues that directors must challenge CISO reports with structured, defensible questions to avoid personal liability and meet their governance obligations. ## Key regulatory and legal context

• APRA CPS 234 establishes ultimate board accountability for information security at regulated entities
• Privacy Act 1988 and state Crimes Act create personal liability risks for directors when personal information protection fails
• ASIC enforcement focus on cyber disclosures and continuous disclosure obligations
• Cyber Security Act 2024 introducing mandatory incident reporting requirements
• Corporations Act director duties around due care and diligence in cyber oversight

## How to respond — key messages

**On specific questions to ask:** Direct clients to the structured questioning framework in the article - focus on business impact, residual risk quantification, and control effectiveness rather than technical details. This creates a documented trail of appropriate oversight.

**On current reporting adequacy:** Most board cyber reports are technically focused and legally insufficient - they need business impact context and clear residual risk statements. A governance review can identify gaps before an incident occurs.

**On personal exposure:** Directors face increasing personal liability through privacy laws and corporations law if they can't demonstrate active, informed oversight. Documented, structured questioning provides legal protection.

**On documentation and process:** Board minutes should reflect specific cyber governance questions and decisions, not just "noted the report." This documentation is critical for regulatory defence.

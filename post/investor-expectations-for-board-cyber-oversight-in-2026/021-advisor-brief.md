## What the article argues
Andy's core claim is that investor scrutiny of board cyber oversight has shifted from informal expectation to a de facto compliance benchmark, driven by the Cyber Security Act 2024 and reinforced by long-standing Corporations Act duties. Boards can no longer rely on passive receipt of management reports; they must demonstrate active, documented governance of cyber risk to satisfy institutional investors like AustralianSuper and BlackRock. The practical consequence is that cyber oversight must move from a risk register footnote to a central, evidenced part of the board's strategic narrative.

## Key regulatory and legal context
- Cyber Security Act 2024, with reporting obligations described in the article as fully enforceable since 1 January 2026.
- Corporations Act 2001, specifically the director duty of care and diligence, linked directly to cyber risk oversight failures.

## How to respond, key messages
- On investor expectations: position this as a documentation and narrative problem as much as a substantive one. Boards may already be asking the right questions, but if it isn't evidenced in minutes and disclosures, it doesn't exist for investor purposes.
- On director duty exposure: frame the Corporations Act link as an intersection, not a new standalone liability regime. The duty of care and diligence hasn't changed; what's changed is the evidentiary bar for showing it was discharged in a digital risk context.
- On CISO vs board role: be direct that operational security reporting and board oversight are different disciplines, and conflating them is the single most common governance gap Andy sees.
- On what "good" looks like: recommend a clear, repeatable pattern, board sets risk appetite, challenges management assumptions, and documents that challenge, rather than a one-off disclosure exercise.
- On assurance: note that independent, board-level review is one credible way to demonstrate rigour to investors, without over-claiming it is legally mandated.

## Advisory angles, services to position
- Board cyber governance health check, benchmarking current oversight practices and disclosures against the pattern investors are reportedly screening for.
- Facilitated board session on setting and articulating cyber risk appetite, distinct from management's technical risk register.
- Review and drafting support for annual report and proxy statement cyber governance disclosures ahead of the next reporting cycle.
- Board minute and papers audit to assess whether existing documentation would withstand investor or regulator scrutiny.
- Director briefing or workshop on the Corporations Act duty of care as it applies to digital risk oversight.

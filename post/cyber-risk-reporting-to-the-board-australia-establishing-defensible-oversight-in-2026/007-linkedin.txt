A board chair received their monthly cyber briefing last Tuesday: "ransomware incidents up 15%, patching at 87%, no breaches detected." The discussion lasted four minutes before moving to the next agenda item.

That same board now faces a fundamental problem. Under the Cyber Security Act 2024, directors carry personal liability for cyber governance failures. Yet most cyber reporting remains operationally focused — incident counts, technical metrics, compliance percentages.

Directors need reporting that connects cyber risk to business continuity, financial impact, and strategic objectives. When a CISO reports "critical vulnerabilities in customer database systems," the board needs to understand exposure in revenue terms, regulatory consequences, and remediation timeframes.

The shift requires moving from status updates to risk-based governance reporting. Instead of "94% of endpoints protected," boards need "unprotected systems in finance division create $2.3M exposure to payment processing disruption."

Defensible oversight means being able to demonstrate you understood the risk, questioned the mitigation strategy, and made informed decisions about acceptable exposure levels.

The question isn't whether your board receives cyber updates — it's whether those updates enable genuine governance or just create the illusion of oversight.

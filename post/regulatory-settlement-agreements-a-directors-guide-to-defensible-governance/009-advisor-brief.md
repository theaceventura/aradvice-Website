## What the article argues

Regulatory settlement agreements are fundamentally different from commercial negotiations because regulators prioritize public deterrence over mutual benefit, meaning boards cannot approach them with traditional compromise strategies. The article contends that directors must understand these agreements are public enforcement tools designed to send market-wide messages, requiring a governance response that emphasizes transparency, accountability, and demonstrated cultural change rather than damage limitation.

## Key regulatory and legal context

• ASIC and APRA settlements are typically accompanied by public statements detailing misconduct and agreement terms
• Regulatory agreements are driven by public interest mandates rather than economic interests
• Settlements serve as deterrence mechanisms for industry-wide behavior modification
• Transparency is generally non-negotiable in regulatory settlement processes

## How to respond — key messages

**Settlement timing**: Early engagement can demonstrate cooperation, but only after ensuring robust governance frameworks are in place and the board can credibly show cultural change. Settlement should never be about avoiding scrutiny but about demonstrating accountability.

**Commercial vs regulatory approach**: Emphasize this is not a negotiation between equals but a regulatory authority exercising statutory powers where the primary goal is industry deterrence, not commercial resolution. The board's response must prioritize transparency and genuine remediation over damage control.

**Director protection**: Personal liability protection comes from demonstrating due diligence, proper oversight frameworks, and genuine efforts to address systemic issues before they escalate. Document decision-making processes and ensure independent advice.

**Governance demonstration**: Implement measurable governance improvements, engage independent expertise, and create audit trails that show systematic cultural and process changes rather than cosmetic responses.

• Director education on regulatory vs commercial dispute management approaches

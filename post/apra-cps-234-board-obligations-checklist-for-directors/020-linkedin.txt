A board chair asked their CISO to present a quarterly cyber risk report. The CISO delivered 47 slides covering threat landscapes, vulnerability scans, and security awareness training metrics. Not one slide addressed whether the bank could meet its APRA CPS 234 obligations if tested tomorrow.

This disconnect reveals why APRA CPS 234 compliance failures persist across Australian financial services. Directors receive operational updates when they need governance assurance. They get activity reports when they need capability assessments.

APRA CPS 234 holds boards accountable for information security capability, not just security activity. The standard demands that directors can demonstrate their institution maintains robust information security arrangements — not that their team attended training or patched servers.

The difference matters when APRA arrives. Operational metrics show effort. Governance frameworks show control. Directors need evidence they can govern effectively, not proof their teams work hard.

Your quarterly cyber reports should answer one question: can we defend our information security governance if APRA examines our board processes next month?

A board receives its quarterly AI governance report: 47 models deployed, 23% accuracy improvement, 8 bias incidents remediated. The chair asks: "Are we legally compliant?" The room falls silent.

Technical metrics don't answer governance questions. Directors need to know whether AI decisions can be explained to affected customers, whether model outputs align with stated company values, and whether incident response procedures actually work under pressure.

The translation happens in three layers: operational dashboards become risk registers, technical testing becomes compliance evidence, and model performance becomes business impact assessment.

Australian directors face the same fiduciary duties for AI decisions as any other business decision. The Corporations Act doesn't create exemptions for algorithmic complexity.

Boards that accept technical reports as governance reports are outsourcing their oversight duties to data scientists who were never appointed to the board.

## How to respond — key messages

**AI metrics for boards:** Focus on business impact metrics like AI system reliability, bias testing results, data quality scores, and compliance with AI policies rather than technical performance metrics. The board needs to see how AI risks translate to operational, reputational and financial risks.

**Adequacy of current reporting:** Most boards are receiving ad-hoc AI updates rather than systematic governance reporting with clear accountabilities, escalation triggers and regular monitoring cycles. **Technical vs governance reporting:** Technical monitoring tracks how AI systems perform; governance reporting tracks how well the organisation manages AI risks through policies, controls, training and incident response.

**Committee structure:** Existing risk committees can handle AI governance if they have the right expertise and reporting frameworks, but complex AI deployments may warrant dedicated oversight structures. **Reporting frequency:** Quarterly reporting on AI governance metrics with immediate escalation for high-risk incidents or policy breaches provides appropriate oversight without micromanagement.

**Documentation requirements:** Board minutes should reflect regular AI governance discussions, management should provide written reports on AI risk metrics, and the board should document its AI risk appetite and governance framework decisions.

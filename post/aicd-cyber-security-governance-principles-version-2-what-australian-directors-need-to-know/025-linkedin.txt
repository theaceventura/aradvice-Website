Picture a chair being handed a cyber dashboard where every metric is green, and asking only one question: "Are we covered?" That question, and management's answer, is the entire problem.

The AICD Cyber Security Governance Principles Version 2, published in November 2024, exist precisely because green dashboards routinely reflect what IT can measure, not what a board is actually liable for. Event logging gaps and legacy infrastructure are the two vulnerabilities most likely to produce a material incident in the current environment, and both are almost invisible in standard management reporting.

The Cyber Security Act 2024 is in force. It does not wait for a board to develop fluency in technical language, and it does not distinguish between directors who were misled by metrics and directors who never asked the right questions.

The updated AICD principles give directors a structured basis to move past passive acceptance of jargon. They establish what accountability structures should look like, what remediation plans should contain, and what questions a director can ask that management cannot deflect with a status colour.

Defensible oversight is not about knowing more than your CISO. It is about knowing enough to test whether what you are being told is complete.

A green dashboard is a reporting artefact. The board's liability is not.

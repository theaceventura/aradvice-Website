A board I know received their AI risk assessment last month: 47 pages covering everything from bias to hallucinations, but zero discussion of how directors would know if any of these risks materialised in their business. The chair's first question was brutal: "How do we tell if this is actually working?"

Most AI governance frameworks hand directors a risk register without the metrics to govern it. You get theoretical harm categories but no practical way to measure whether your AI systems are performing within acceptable parameters or drifting toward the boundaries of your risk appetite.

The disconnect creates a governance vacuum. Directors approve AI deployments based on upfront assessments, then rely on management reports that may not surface emerging risks until they become incidents. By then, demonstrating reasonable care becomes significantly harder.

Defensible AI oversight in 2026 requires boards to define observable thresholds for each AI risk they accept. Not just what could go wrong, but what early warning signals look like and who watches for them.

The question isn't whether your organisation uses AI responsibly—it's whether you can prove you governed it deliberately.

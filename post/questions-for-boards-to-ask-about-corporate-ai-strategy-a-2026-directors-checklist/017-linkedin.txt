A board receives a proposal to deploy AI for fraud detection across customer transactions. The technology promises 40% cost reduction and real-time monitoring capabilities. The CEO recommends immediate implementation to stay competitive.

The finance committee approves the business case. The risk committee notes cyber security considerations. The audit committee discusses data governance implications. Yet no single committee owns the complete AI governance picture.

This fragmentation creates board-level blind spots. Directors end up approving AI initiatives without understanding algorithmic bias risks, model validation requirements, or regulatory compliance obligations across the entire AI lifecycle.

The solution isn't another committee. It's equipping every director with the right questions to ask before any AI decision reaches the boardroom. Questions that cut through vendor promises and management enthusiasm to reveal the governance substance underneath.

The AI governance gap isn't technical. It's structural — and it sits at board level.

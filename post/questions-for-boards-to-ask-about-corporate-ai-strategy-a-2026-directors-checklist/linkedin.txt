The CRO stood up in the boardroom and said: "We've integrated AI into our fraud detection, customer service, and procurement processes over the past 18 months. ROI is tracking at 23%." When the chair asked about governance frameworks, the silence lasted twelve seconds.

This disconnect between AI deployment and AI oversight is becoming the norm. Australian boards are approving AI budgets and celebrating efficiency gains while fundamental governance questions remain unasked.

The questions that matter aren't about the technology itself. They're about decision-making transparency, accountability chains when AI systems fail, and whether your organisation can demonstrate reasonable care in AI deployment if regulators or shareholders come calling.

Directors who think AI governance can wait until the technology matures have it backwards. The governance framework needs to precede the deployment, not follow it.

The most dangerous assumption in the boardroom today is that someone else is handling the AI governance while you focus on the AI returns.

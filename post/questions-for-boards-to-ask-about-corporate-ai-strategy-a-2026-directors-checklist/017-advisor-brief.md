## How to respond, key messages
- **Legal exposure:** Confirm the duty of care and diligence under the Corporations Act applies regardless of whether a decision is delegated to an algorithm; stepping stones liability is a real ASIC enforcement pattern (established in other contexts) now being extended into AI oversight commentary, so the risk is credible, not hypothetical, though no AI-specific case law exists yet.
- **Personal liability:** Frame stepping stones liability simply as failure to identify and act on a foreseeable risk creates a foundation for later findings against directors, even if the initial AI failure was operational rather than a board decision.
- **Governance structure:** Full board ownership of AI risk appetite is the right call; sub-committees can prepare analysis but the board must own the sign-off, consistent with how other emerging risks (cyber, climate) have matured.
- **Framework adequacy:** Off-the-shelf policies are a starting point at best; the framework needs to be defensible under regulatory scrutiny, meaning documented rationale, traceable decisions, and evidence of board-level challenge, not just adoption of a template.
- **Practical next step:** Recommend directors table structured questions (data provenance, legal right to use, accountability for outcomes) formally in board papers, and require documented executive responses rather than verbal reassurance.
